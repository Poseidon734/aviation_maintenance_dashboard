# ✈️ Aviation Reliability & Maintenance KPIs

This repository utilizes the `aviation_fault_maintenance_dataset_enriched_2.csv` dataset to calculate, monitor, and predict key industry-standard reliability indicators. These metrics are essential for Continuing Airworthiness Management Organization (CAMO) and Maintenance Control Centre (MCC) operations to ensure fleet safety, optimize maintenance schedules, and improve aircraft availability.

Below is a detailed breakdown of the aeronautical reliability indicators generated by our analytical model.

---

## 1. Dispatch Reliability Rate (%)

**Definition:** 
The Dispatch Reliability Rate measures the percentage of revenue flights that depart without being delayed or canceled due to technical issues. It is the primary indicator of the technical health of a fleet and the effectiveness of the maintenance program.

**Calculation:**
$$ \text{Dispatch Reliability (\%)} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Scheduled Flights} - \text{Technical Delays} - \text{Technical Cancellations}}{\text{Total Scheduled Flights}} \right) \times 100 $$

**Fields Used from Dataset:** 
* `Fleet_Avg_Monthly_Departures` / `Total_Departures` (Denominator)
* `Is_Technical_Delay` (Numerator subtraction)

---

## 2. Technical Delay Rate (per 100 or 1,000 Departures)

**Definition:** 
This metric tracks the frequency of technical delays relative to the number of departures. Following IATA standards, a technical delay is typically defined as a delay exceeding 15 minutes caused by an aircraft system malfunction or maintenance action. Normalizing this rate (usually per 100 departures for smaller fleets, or 1,000 for larger ones) allows for benchmarking across different fleet sizes.

**Calculation:**
$$ \text{Technical Delay Rate} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Technical Delays}}{\text{Total Departures}} \right) \times 100 $$

**Fields Used from Dataset:** 
* `Delay_Minutes` (to determine if $> 15$ mins)
* `Is_Technical_Delay`
* `Total_Departures`

---

## 3. Component Reliability: URR & MTBUR

**Definition:** 
These metrics evaluate the reliability and operational lifespan of specific rotable components or line replaceable units (LRUs).
* **Unscheduled Removal Rate (URR):** The frequency of premature, unplanned removals of a component per 1,000 flight hours. 
* **Mean Time Between Unscheduled Removals (MTBUR):** The average number of flight hours a component accumulates before failing or requiring an unscheduled removal.

**Calculation:**
$$ \text{URR} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Unscheduled Removals}}{\text{Total Component Flight Hours}} \right) \times 1000 $$

$$ \text{MTBUR (Flight Hours)} = \frac{\text{Total Fleet Flight Hours}}{\text{Total Unscheduled Removals for that Component}} $$

**Fields Used from Dataset:** 
* `Unscheduled_Removal` (Boolean flag)
* `Part_Number` and `Serial_Number`
* `Cumulative_Flight_Hours`

---

## 4. Repetitive Defect Detection (3-in-5 / 3-in-10 Rule)

**Definition:** 
A repetitive defect (or chronic aircraft issue) occurs when the same technical failure happens multiple times on the same aircraft tail number within a short timeframe. Standard industry thresholds for flagging these are usually:
* 3 occurrences of the same ATA chapter fault within 5 consecutive days.
* 3 occurrences of the same ATA chapter fault within 10 consecutive flight cycles.

**Calculation / Logic:**
The system groups records by `Aircraft_ID` and `ATA_Chapter`, orders them chronologically by `Event_Date`, and calculates the time ($\Delta t$) or cycles ($\Delta c$) between the 1st and 3rd occurrence. If $\Delta t \leq 5 \text{ days}$, an alert is triggered.

**Fields Used from Dataset:** 
* `Aircraft_ID`
* `ATA_Chapter`
* `Event_Date`
* `Cumulative_Flight_Cycles`

---

## 5. ATA Chapter Pareto Analysis (80/20 Rule)

**Definition:** 
The Pareto analysis is a quality control tool that identifies the "vital few" systems causing the most disruptions. Based on the Pareto Principle, approximately 80% of technical delays or defects are typically caused by 20% of the aircraft systems (categorized by ATA Chapters). This helps prioritize engineering investigations and maintenance budget allocation.

**Calculation:**
Cumulative percentage of defects sorted descending by ATA Chapter count:
$$ \text{Cumulative \%} = \frac{\sum \text{Defects}_{i}}{\text{Total Fleet Defects}} \times 100 $$

**Fields Used from Dataset:** 
* `ATA_Chapter`
* `Event_ID` or total record counts

---

## 6. Control Threshold Alerts (Upper Control Limit - UCL)

**Definition:** 
Reliability alert levels define the maximum acceptable defect rate for a specific aircraft system based on historical performance. If the current defect rate exceeds the Upper Control Limit (UCL), the system is considered out of statistical control, triggering a mandatory engineering review. The UCL is typically calculated at 2 or 3 standard deviations (Sigma) above the historical mean.

**Calculation:**
$$ \text{UCL (3 Sigma)} = \mu + 3\sigma $$
Where $\mu$ is the historical average monthly defect rate for an ATA chapter, and $\sigma$ is the standard deviation.

**Fields Used from Dataset:** 
* `ATA_Chapter`
* `Event_Date` (Grouped by Month/Quarter)
* Rolling defect counts
